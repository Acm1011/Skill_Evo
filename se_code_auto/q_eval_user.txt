EVALUATION CHECKLIST (INTERNAL USE ONLY)
--------------------------------

Use the following checklist to guide your internal evaluation.  
You must NOT expose any per-criterion scores or verbal explanations in your output.

1. Mathematical Truthfulness & Internal Consistency  
   - Check if all given equations, relationships, and conditions are mathematically correct and realistic.  
   - Look for contradictions, impossible constraints, or obviously false “given” statements.  
   - Consider whether the structure makes sense (no undefined symbols, no impossible equalities, no nonsense data).

   Internal scale (for your reasoning only):
   - 4 = Fully consistent and correct; no serious issues.  
   - 3 = Generally correct; at most minor or subtle issues.  
   - 2 = Some questionable or imprecise parts, but the main problem is still mostly salvageable.  
   - 1 = Serious doubts about correctness or feasibility.  
   - 0 = Clearly incorrect or self-contradictory; not mathematically valid as stated.

2. Difficulty & Depth of Reasoning  
   - Assume the target audience is **advanced high-school students preparing for math contests**.  
   - Higher scores should correspond to **harder** problems that require **nontrivial insight**.  
   - Check whether the solution requires at least one clever or non-standard step (e.g., smart substitution, invariants, symmetry, bounding, non-obvious case split, etc.), beyond routine plugging or standard formulas.  
   - If the problem is trivial, straightforward plug-and-chug, or purely formula-based, the difficulty score should be low.

   Internal scale (for your reasoning only):
   - 4 = Very challenging; significantly fewer than ~25% of strong contest students would solve it; clearly requires deep insight.  
   - 3 = Solid contest-level; nontrivial and requires some insight, but still accessible to many well-trained students.  
   - 2 = Moderate; requires some thought but closer to a difficult textbook exercise than to a true contest problem.  
   - 1 = Mostly routine; can be solved by straightforward standard methods.  
   - 0 = Trivial or nearly trivial.

3. Well-posedness & Answer Uniqueness  
   - Check whether the question is clearly asking for a well-defined object:  
     - a specific number, expression, or a clearly described finite set of solutions.  
   - Verify that, under the stated conditions, it is reasonable to expect **essentially one correct answer** (or one clearly specified finite set).  
   - Make sure important domains (real, integer, positive, etc.) and constraints are specified so that the solution set is not ambiguous.

   Internal scale (for your reasoning only):
   - 1 = Well-posed with essentially unique answer; no important ambiguity.  
   - 0 = Ill-posed, ambiguous, or likely to have multiple essentially different answers.

4. Clarity & Practical Solvability (Minor Factor)  
   - Is the problem statement understandable and self-contained?  
   - Are all variables and objects defined clearly enough for a good student to attempt it?  
   - Is it realistically solvable with advanced high-school knowledge (no need for university-level tools like advanced calculus, linear algebra, or measure theory)?

   Internal scale (for your reasoning only):
   - 1 = Clear and self-contained; realistically solvable.  
   - 0 = Noticeable issues with clarity or missing definitions; or requires tools far beyond high-school contest level.
